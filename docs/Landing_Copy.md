How to Use This Document

This document contains the actual copy for the GlowRank Phase 1 MVP landing page, intake form, payment screen, and email sequence — across **two parallel acquisition tracks**: men 18–35 (dating-motivated) and women 25–45 (returning daters / new chapter). The copy is calibrated to the tone rules in the *Trust, Safety & Tone Guidelines* document — warm, specific, never deficit-based.

**Why two tracks:** the same product serves both audiences, but they arrive in different emotional states, respond to different hooks, and need different reassurances. A single homepage with one headline will under-convert for at least one audience. The implementation pattern is a personalization fork after the ad click — TikTok creative tagged by audience routes to the appropriate landing variant.

**Copy testing principle:** *Test headlines and hooks against conversion. Never test tone-violating copy "just to see." Once a deficit-based ad runs, you've trained your funnel to attract users in a worse emotional state, and the entire downstream experience degrades.*

1\. Landing Page Architecture

1.1 Implementation pattern

- **Ad creative is gender-targeted** on TikTok/Meta and routes to one of two landing variants via UTM parameter.

- **Variant A** (/m or /?v=m): men's track, dating-motivated framing.

- **Variant B** (/w or /?v=w): women's track, new-chapter framing.

- **Variant C** (/) — neutral default for organic traffic and referrals. Lighter framing, asks gender first.

- **The intake form, payment, and report flow are identical across variants.** Only the marketing surface differs.

1.2 Why not a single homepage

A unified homepage either picks one audience (alienating the other) or hedges (converts poorly for both). The cost of running two simple landing variants is low — both can be built in Framer in a day — and the conversion lift will be material. Once the product reaches Phase 2, a single brand homepage can hand off to segment-specific subpages.

3\. Variant B — Women's Track

3.1 Positioning context

**Who this is for:** Women 25–45 entering a new chapter — post-breakup, post-divorce, returning to dating, post-career-pause, or anyone wanting to feel intentional about how they show up. The framing is *renewal*, not *inadequacy*. Not for wedding prep (Phase 2), not for general beauty (too broad).

**Tone discipline:** *This audience is hyper-attuned to brands that exploit insecurity. The copy must read as a smart friend with taste, not as a beauty algorithm with a payment screen. If a headline could plausibly appear in a fashion magazine alongside Aesop or Glossier copy, it's on-tone. If it feels like a weight-loss ad, it's off.*

*Recommended starting test: B1 vs B4. B1 is emotionally aspirational; B4 is specific and curiosity-driven. Both avoid deficit framing.*

A short looping animation showing a sample report being generated — but with a different aesthetic register than the men's variant. Editorial photography aesthetic (soft natural light, neutral palette, no faces) rather than the men's variant's slightly punchier visual style. Avoid pink-coded design clichés.

3.4 Tone guarantee section (women's variant)

3.6 Specific reassurances for women's variant

Women in this segment have specific learned objections from prior beauty-tech experiences. Address them directly in a sub-section:

- **"We don't use anti-aging language."** Aging is not a problem to be solved.

- **"We never recommend procedures."** No Botox, fillers, surgery, or threading suggestions. Ever.

- **"We don't talk about weight."** Body recommendations are tied to your stated goals and styling — not to numbers on a scale.

- **"Your photos are deleted in 30 days."** Not used for training. Not shared.

9\. Ad Creative Briefs — Dual Track

**Cross-reference:** Read Section 9 of the Trust & Safety document before producing any ad creative. The allowed and banned angles are non-negotiable.

9.1 Men's track creatives

M1: "The 3 things"

- **Hook (first 1.5s):** "AI told me the 3 things to fix first."

- **Body (5–10s):** Quick cuts showing a sample report on phone. Hand scrolling through priorities.

- **CTA (last 2s):** "\$10, link in bio."

M2: "vs. the stylist"

- **Hook:** "\$10 GlowRank vs. \$300 stylist. Honest review."

- **Body:** Person scrolling a real report on screen, subtle reactions of "oh that's a good one."

M3: "I was nervous"

- **Hook:** "Was nervous to let AI look at my photos. This was actually... helpful?"

- **Body:** Genuine first-person reaction. Highlights the tone.

9.2 Women's track creatives

W1: "What I learned about my own face"

- **Hook:** "AI told me something about my own face I'd never noticed."

- **Body:** Editorial-style cuts. Voiceover talks through what was specific about the recommendation. Tone is curious, not insecure.

- **CTA:** "Try it. \$10, link in bio."

W2: "Less product, more polish"

- **Hook:** "AI said I'm using too much. Turns out it was right."

- **Body:** Before/after of a simplified makeup or skincare routine. Quick. Calm. Aesthetic-led.

- **CTA:** "Get your edit. Link below."

W3: "Returning to dating"

- **Hook:** "Back on the apps after 4 years. AI gave me my pre-dating game plan."

- **Body:** Personal, voiceover-led. References the new-chapter framing directly.

- **CTA:** "\$10 and 90 seconds. Link in bio."

9.3 Banned creative concepts (both tracks)

- Anything implying the user is currently unattractive.

- Before/after using real users' faces without explicit written marketing consent.

- Comparisons that frame the "before" as ugly or shameful.

- Anti-aging language in either track.

- Weight loss framing in either track.

- Looksmaxxing vocabulary in either track.

- Pink-coded design clichés in women's creatives (talk down to the audience).

9.4 Testing framework

- **Week 1:** Run M1–M3 and W1–W3 simultaneously at \$25–\$50/day each.

- **Week 2:** Cut bottom 2 performers across both tracks. Reallocate budget.

- **Week 3+:** Always have 1 new creative in test per track. Fatigue is real.

- **Track-level read:** By end of Week 4, you'll have a sense of whether one track converts materially better than the other. Don't kill the lower-converting track unless the gap is \>2x — segment-balanced acquisition is the strategic goal.

—

*The dual-track approach doubles the surface area of the marketing operation but keeps the product underneath identical. The discipline is: never let one track's tone drift to please conversion. The brand is the product.*
